1. Learn

Instead of repeating a person's name, English often uses a pronoun.

Ben plays football.
He plays football.
I can see Ben.
I can see him.
Ben's bicycle is red.
His bicycle is red.
DOERhe · she · they
Who does the action?
RECEIVERhim · her · them
Who receives the action?
OWNERhis · her · their
Who owns something?

Important: Do not choose a word because it “sounds familiar.” Ask what job it is doing in the sentence.

2. Quick examples

She likes dogs. → Lucy does the action.
Ben helped her. → Lucy receives the action.
Her dog is brown. → The dog belongs to Lucy.
They are playing. / Mum called them. / Their bikes are outside.

The Lost Backpack

On Sunday morning, Emma went to the park with her brother, Sam. Emma took her new backpack with her. It had some sandwiches and a bottle of water inside.

Emma and Sam met their friends, Lucy and Tom, near a big tree. They played together for an hour.

Later, Emma wanted a sandwich, but she could not find her backpack.

“Did you see it?” she asked Sam.

Sam looked behind the tree and found the backpack. He picked it up and gave it to his sister.

Emma thanked him. Then they all sat under the tree and ate their lunch.
