Week 1 – Lesson 5: Possession & Story Tracking

my/mine · your/yours · his · her/hers · our/ours · their/theirs · story evidence

1. Learn

This is her bag.
This bag is hers.

If a noun comes immediately after the possessive word, use my, your, his, her, our, their.

If the possessive word stands alone, use mine, yours, his, hers, ours, theirs.

Quick check: Look at the next word. If a noun comes next, ask which owner word can go before that noun.

Story tracking

Mia lost her scarf. Tom found it near the gate. He gave it back to her.

To understand it, he, her, they, them, follow the meaning of the whole story. Do not automatically choose the nearest noun.

Useful collocations

lose something · find something · look for something · give something back · pick something up · belong to someone

3. Reading Checkpoint

The Missing Camera

On Sunday afternoon, Leo and his cousin Amy went to a wildlife park with their grandparents. Amy carried a small camera in her backpack because she wanted to take pictures of the animals.

At lunchtime, the family sat near a lake. Leo took his sandwich out of his bag, while Amy put her camera on the bench beside her. After lunch, they walked to the bird area.

A few minutes later, Amy stopped. “I can’t find my camera,” she said. Leo remembered seeing it on the bench, so he ran back to the lake. The camera was still there. A park worker had picked it up and put it on a table.

Leo thanked the worker and took the camera back to Amy. “Thanks! I thought I had lost it,” she said. Then Amy took a photo of Leo and their grandparents.
